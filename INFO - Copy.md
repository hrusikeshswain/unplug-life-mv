AI-powered personal wellbeing assistant

You could introduce a conversational assistant that answers questions such as:
- Why do I feel anxious without my phone?
- Why do I check WhatsApp every five minutes?
- How can I stop watching Reels at night?
- How can I help my child stop demanding a phone?
- How can I avoid checking office messages after work?
- How can I help my parents develop healthy phone habits?
The AI should provide educational guidance, not diagnose mental health conditions or replace a psychologist.

14. India-specific opportunities
I would strongly recommend developing this application with Indian users in mind.

India has a particularly interesting opportunity because smartphone usage spans different income groups, education levels, languages, and generations.

A 2025 systematic review of Indian studies estimated a pooled nomophobia prevalence of 77.6% among the included adult study participants. However, the studies were highly heterogeneous, and many participants were university students. This must not be presented as the prevalence among all Indians. 
PMC+1


Consider these features:
- English, Hindi, Odia, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, and other Indian languages.
- Voice-based guidance for people who are not comfortable reading.
- Simple interfaces for senior citizens.
- Affordable, low-data content.
- Offline learning materials.
- Regional examples and culturally relevant activities.
- Family-based digital wellbeing programs.
- School and college awareness programs.
- Community workshops.
One particularly interesting opportunity is to create regional-language awareness videos that explain nomophobia using everyday situations.
